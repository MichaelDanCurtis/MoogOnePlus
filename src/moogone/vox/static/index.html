What you are about to hear partials at fc ± n·fm, amplitude Jn(index)

Green is the carrier. Amber are ordinary sidebands. Blue are reflected — their frequency came out negative and folded back up, so they land on top of other partials. That is usually why a small ratio change sounds much bigger than it looks. Verified against the instrument: every partial predicted here was found in a recording of its real output.

Nothing crosses the network but the notes. The FM engine and the sampler already make their sound inside the instrument, so engaging a send only changes where they write and puts one of Moog's own effects in front of the jacks. It runs on buses nothing else uses, so no patch of yours is touched — bypass and everything is exactly as it was.

Patcher build an effect · it becomes a SynthDef

Generated SuperCollider compiled by a real sclang when you press Run

The main outputs are buses 0 and 7, not 0 and 1, and Out writes consecutive buses — so a stereo signal into Out bus 0 puts its right channel somewhere that never leaves the instrument. Use two Out nodes, one on 0 and one on 7. Out adds to a bus, so a patch that ends in Out layers over the instrument; read In and write back to make an insert.

press Generate

This machine has no SuperCollider to compile against, so Vox emits readable source rather than a compiled SynthDef. Check it by eye, paste it into SuperCollider, or send it to the instrument's own sclang. Pushing compiled bytes over /d_recv waits on a reference compiler to verify against.

Ask AI it proposes; you apply

The model is given the live node tree and returns a proposal. Every change is re-checked against the tree before anything is sent — unknown controls are dropped and values are clamped to each control's own range. Nothing reaches the synth until you press Apply.
